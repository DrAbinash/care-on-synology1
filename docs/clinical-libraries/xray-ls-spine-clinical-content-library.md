# X-Ray Lumbosacral Spine Clinical Content Library

## 1. Views / Adequacy
Support AP, lateral, right/left oblique, flexion, extension, spot L5-S1 and other/manual views.
State limitations when the lumbosacral junction, posterior elements or required anatomy is inadequately visualized.

## 2. Normal LS Spine
FINDINGS:
Lumbar lordosis and alignment are maintained. Vertebral body heights and intervertebral disc spaces are preserved. No acute fracture or listhesis is identified. Visualized posterior elements are intact. Sacroiliac joints show no gross radiographic abnormality.

IMPRESSION:
No acute osseous abnormality identified on the provided lumbosacral spine radiographs.

## 3. Numbering / Transitional Anatomy
- Standard numbering.
- Transitional lumbosacral vertebra.
- Sacralization.
- Lumbarization.
- Lowest prominent lumbar-type vertebra designated L5.
- Numbering based on available whole-spine/localizer information.
- Manual designation.

Never silently assume the lowest mobile segment is L5-S1 when transitional anatomy is present.

## 4. Alignment
- Preserved lumbar lordosis.
- Straightening/loss of lordosis.
- Hyperlordosis.
- Dextro/levoconvex scoliosis.
- Anterolisthesis / retrolisthesis.
- Lateral translation.

## 5. Vertebral Bodies / Endplates
- Heights maintained.
- Compression/wedging/collapse.
- Cortical disruption.
- Marginal osteophytes.
- Endplate sclerosis/irregularity.
- Schmorl-node appearance when visible.
- Focal lytic/sclerotic/mixed lesion.

## 6. Disc Spaces
T12-L1 when included; L1-2 through L5-S1:
- preserved
- mild/moderate/severe reduction
- vacuum phenomenon
- endplate sclerosis
- osteophytes

## 7. Posterior Elements / Facets / Pars
- Facet arthropathy/hypertrophy.
- Pars defect/spondylolysis, unilateral/bilateral.
- Posterior-element abnormality.
Do not diagnose a pars defect from an equivocal projection alone.

## 8. Spondylolisthesis
Support:
- level
- direction
- measured translation
- percentage slip when measured
- Meyerding grade when validly derived
- pars status
- radiologist-selected degenerative/isthmic/other interpretation

Do not infer etiology from slip alone.

## 9. Degenerative Lumbar Spondylosis
- Multilevel marginal osteophytes.
- Disc-space reduction at [levels].
- Endplate sclerosis.
- Facet arthropathy.
- Vacuum phenomenon.
- Degenerative listhesis.

Suggested impression:
Multilevel lumbar spondylotic/degenerative changes, most pronounced at [levels].

## 10. Dynamic Flexion / Extension
Only when performed:
- neutral/flexion/extension translation
- change in translation
- angular change
- level
- motion limitation
- radiologist-selected instability

Do not apply an unreviewed hard-coded instability threshold.

## 11. Trauma
- Compression fracture.
- Wedge fracture.
- Burst-pattern suspicion.
- Vertebral height loss percentage when measured.
- Cortical disruption.
- Malalignment.
- Retropulsion only when visible.
- Posterior-element injury.

Plain radiography should not overstate spinal canal, neural or ligamentous injury. CT/MRI recommendation may be selected.

## 12. Deformity / Scoliosis
- Dextro/levoconvex curvature.
- Apex.
- Cobb angle when deliberately measured on an appropriate radiograph.
- Coronal imbalance when supported.
- Vertebral rotation description when selected.

## 13. Sacroiliac Joints
- Preserved.
- Sclerosis.
- Irregularity.
- Joint-space narrowing.
- Ankylosis.
- Asymmetric abnormality.

Do not automatically diagnose inflammatory sacroiliitis or axial spondyloarthritis from nonspecific sclerosis.

## 14. Transitional Vertebra
- Enlarged transverse process.
- Unilateral/bilateral pseudoarticulation.
- Fusion.
- Sacralization/lumbarization.
- Castellvi classification when adequately demonstrated and radiologist-selected.

Do not automatically diagnose Bertolotti syndrome from transitional anatomy alone.

## 15. Infection / Destructive Pattern
- Disc-space loss.
- Adjacent endplate irregularity/destruction.
- Vertebral collapse.
- Paravertebral soft-tissue abnormality when visible.
Radiography may raise suspicion; etiologic diagnosis requires correlation.

## 16. Tumor / Osseous Lesion
- Focal lytic/sclerotic/mixed lesion.
- Multifocal lesions.
- Pathological fracture pattern.
- Pedicle/posterior-element abnormality when visible.
Do not automatically diagnose metastasis from indeterminate radiographic lesions.

## 17. Post-operative
- Laminectomy.
- Posterior fusion.
- Pedicle screws/rods.
- Interbody cage/spacer.
- Vertebroplasty/kyphoplasty.
- Alignment.
- Gross hardware continuity.
- Breakage/migration.
- Convincing perihardware lucency.
- Adjacent-segment degeneration.

Do not automatically diagnose hardware loosening from equivocal lucency.

## 18. Impression Seeds
- No acute osseous abnormality on provided views.
- Multilevel lumbar spondylotic changes, most pronounced at [levels].
- [Direction] listhesis of [vertebra] over [vertebra], with measurement/grade when available.
- Compression fracture of [vertebra] with approximately [X]% height loss.
- Transitional lumbosacral anatomy as described; numbering caution applies.
- Post-operative changes as described.
