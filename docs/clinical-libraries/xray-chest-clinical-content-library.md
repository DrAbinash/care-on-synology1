# X-Ray Chest Clinical Content Library

## 1. Technique / Quality
Normal:
- PA erect chest radiograph with adequate inspiration and exposure.
- Cardiomediastinal silhouette is within normal limits for projection.
- Both lungs are adequately expanded.

Technique variants:
- AP erect / AP supine / portable AP / lateral / lordotic projection.
- Low-volume film with crowding of bronchovascular markings.
- Patient rotation limits assessment of mediastinal width and hilar symmetry.
- Underpenetration / overpenetration / motion artifact / incomplete inclusion of apices or costophrenic angles.

Safety:
- AP magnification can exaggerate cardiac size.
- Technical limitation should be stated when it materially affects interpretation.

## 2. Normal Chest
FINDINGS:
Trachea is central. Cardiomediastinal silhouette is within normal limits for the projection. Bilateral lung fields are clear without focal air-space opacity. Pulmonary vascular markings are within normal limits. Hila are not enlarged. Costophrenic angles are clear. No pleural effusion or pneumothorax. Visualized osseous structures show no acute abnormality.

IMPRESSION:
No active cardiopulmonary abnormality identified on this radiograph.

## 3. Air-space Disease / Pneumonia Pattern
- Focal / multifocal air-space opacity involving the [right/left] [upper/mid/lower] zone.
- Lobar/segmental consolidation with / without air bronchograms.
- Patchy peribronchial air-space opacities.
- Cavitary change within an area of consolidation.
- Associated pleural effusion.
- Interval increased / decreased / resolved opacity compared with prior radiograph.

Suggested impression:
- Air-space opacity/consolidation in the [location], suspicious for infective/inflammatory change in the appropriate clinical setting.
Do not automatically assign a microbiologic etiology from radiography alone.

## 4. Tuberculosis-pattern Observations
- Upper-zone predominant heterogeneous opacity.
- Fibro-cavitary change.
- Apical pleuroparenchymal thickening.
- Volume loss with hilar elevation.
- Calcified granuloma.
- Miliary-pattern innumerable tiny nodules.
- Hilar/mediastinal lymph-node enlargement pattern when visible.

Use descriptive imaging language. Active tuberculosis must be radiologist-selected and clinically/microbiologically correlated.

## 5. Pulmonary Edema / Heart Failure Pattern
- Cardiomegaly when projection/technique permits.
- Pulmonary vascular redistribution/congestion.
- Bilateral perihilar/interstitial or air-space opacities.
- Septal/interstitial lines.
- Bilateral pleural effusions.
- Peribronchial cuffing.

Suggested impression:
- Radiographic features of pulmonary vascular/interstitial edema.
Do not infer cardiac failure solely from one nonspecific opacity.

## 6. Pleural Disease
- Blunting of [right/left] costophrenic angle.
- Small / moderate / large pleural effusion.
- Loculated pleural opacity.
- Pleural thickening / calcification.
- Hydropneumothorax with air-fluid level.
- No pleural effusion.

## 7. Pneumothorax
- Visceral pleural line with absent peripheral lung markings on the [right/left].
- Small / moderate / large pneumothorax.
- Associated partial lung collapse.
- Mediastinal shift and depressed hemidiaphragm may raise concern for tension physiology in the appropriate setting.

Do not label tension pneumothorax from size alone; use radiographic signs and clinical context.

## 8. Collapse / Atelectasis
- Linear/subsegmental atelectatic band.
- Lobar/segmental volume loss.
- Elevated hemidiaphragm.
- Fissural displacement.
- Hilar/mediastinal shift toward volume loss.
- Compensatory hyperinflation.

## 9. COPD / Emphysema Pattern
- Hyperinflated lungs.
- Flattened diaphragms.
- Increased retrosternal clear space on lateral view.
- Attenuated peripheral vascular markings.
- Bullous change.
- Chronic bronchitic/peribronchial markings.

Do not automatically diagnose COPD from hyperinflation alone.

## 10. ILD / Diffuse Lung Disease
- Bilateral reticular / reticulonodular / interstitial opacities.
- Upper / lower / peripheral / perihilar predominance.
- Reduced lung volumes.
- Coarse fibrotic change.
- Honeycomb-pattern lucencies where radiographically evident.

Radiography may suggest diffuse interstitial lung disease; CT characterization may be recommended when appropriate.

## 11. Nodule / Mass
- Solitary / multiple pulmonary nodule(s).
- Rounded/irregular pulmonary opacity measuring approximately [X] mm/cm.
- Side, zone and relation to hilum/pleura.
- Calcification when visible.
- Mass-like opacity.

Do not characterize benignity/malignancy beyond what radiography supports. CT correlation may be selected.

## 12. Cardiac / Mediastinal
- Cardiac silhouette enlarged / not enlarged for projection.
- Aortic unfolding / atherosclerotic calcification.
- Mediastinal widening.
- Hilar prominence.
- Pulmonary artery prominence.
- Right/left paratracheal opacity.
- Tracheal deviation.

## 13. Diaphragm / Upper Abdomen
- Elevated [right/left] hemidiaphragm.
- Flattened diaphragms.
- Eventration pattern.
- Subdiaphragmatic free gas.
- Retrocardiac air-fluid level / hiatus hernia pattern when visible.

## 14. Trauma
- Rib fracture [side/level] when visible.
- Clavicular fracture.
- Pneumothorax / hemothorax pattern.
- Pulmonary contusion pattern.
- Subcutaneous emphysema.
- Mediastinal widening.

A normal chest radiograph does not exclude occult thoracic injury.

## 15. Lines / Tubes / Devices
Support:
- Endotracheal tube: tip position relative to carina when measurable.
- Enteric tube: course below diaphragm and distal tip when included.
- Central venous catheter / PICC: side, course, tip location.
- Intercostal drain: side, course, tip/side-hole visibility.
- Pacemaker / ICD: generator, lead course, gross continuity.
- Prosthetic valve / sternotomy wires / other hardware.

Use descriptive position. Do not invent tip distances or declare vascular placement without adequate visualization.

## 16. Pediatric Chest
- Lung expansion.
- Perihilar/peribronchial markings.
- Focal air-space opacity.
- Thymic contour when age-appropriate.
- Pleural abnormality.
- Cardiomediastinal silhouette appropriate for projection/age.

## 17. Post-operative / ICU
- Post-sternotomy change.
- Post-thoracic surgery volume loss.
- Surgical clips/staples.
- Lines/tubes/devices.
- Pleural air/fluid.
- Interval comparison.

## 18. Comparison Vocabulary
- New.
- Increased.
- Mildly increased.
- Decreased.
- Improving.
- Resolved.
- Unchanged / stable.
- Interval development.
- Interval removal/repositioning of device.

Only use comparison language when a prior study is actually available.

## 19. Critical-pattern Library
Potential urgent observations:
- Pneumothorax with tension-pattern signs.
- New large pneumothorax.
- Large pleural effusion with major mass effect.
- Subdiaphragmatic free gas.
- Major acute device malposition.
- Marked pulmonary edema when clinically urgent.
- Major traumatic abnormality.

Critical communication must be documented only through the existing CARE critical-result workflow.

## 20. Impression Seeds
- No active cardiopulmonary abnormality.
- Focal [right/left] [zone/lobe] air-space opacity/consolidation.
- Bilateral pulmonary interstitial/air-space edema pattern.
- [Right/left] pleural effusion.
- [Right/left] pneumothorax.
- [Lobar] collapse/atelectatic change.
- Hyperinflation/emphysematous change.
- Bilateral diffuse interstitial pulmonary opacities.
- Pulmonary nodule/mass-like opacity requiring further characterization as selected by the radiologist.
- Support devices as described.
